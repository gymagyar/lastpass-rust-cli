Two shared-folder RSA key records decode to 16 and 22 bytes, whereas the supplied
C implementation and LastPass AES-256 shared-folder format require 32 bytes.
Twelve account records follow invalid shared keys and could not be decrypted.
There is no claim that the entire vault was successfully read. The Rust CLI
warns explicitly, counts those unavailable records, and omits them from results.
The C parser rejects those folder keys too, then falls back to the personal key;
that fallback did not decrypt any of the 12 affected records during verification.

The permissive live test verifies all accessible items and reports unavailable
counts. With `LPASS_TEST_REQUIRE_COMPLETE=1`, it fails for this account because
of the 12 unavailable records. This distinguishes successful protocol/read
workflows from complete coverage of the account's damaged shared-folder data.

The live tests made no item edits, creations, removals, share invitations,
permission changes, imports, or master-password changes. Attachments were tested
with synthetic data, without live attachment downloads. Logout acceptance
matches the C client for a 301 response; no redirect is followed, and the CLI
clears its local state. Remote token invalidation after that response was not
independently checked.
